# Evidence checklist

A strong lab report connects each finding to something a reviewer can inspect.

## Before marking a lab complete

- [ ] Record the date and tools actually used.
- [ ] Explain the objective in your own words.
- [ ] Save the commands or queries you ran.
- [ ] Include readable screenshots or text output.
- [ ] Describe what the results mean, including uncertainty.
- [ ] Describe a mistake, troubleshooting step, or lesson learned.
- [ ] State whether the data is synthetic, a public dataset, or a capture from your own lab.
- [ ] Credit tutorials, datasets, and AI assistance that materially helped.

## Publishing evidence

Put screenshots in an `evidence/` folder inside the lab. Use descriptive names such as `failure-counts.png`. Caption each screenshot so a reviewer knows what it proves. Text output is also valid evidence.

Check real captures for passwords, tokens, personal information, and details you do not intend to publish. The bundled synthetic CSV contains fictional accounts and documentation IP addresses.

## Linking from a résumé

Portfolio URL: https://github.com/CyberSecJase/Cybersecurity-Homelabs-

While learning, label it **Cybersecurity Lab Portfolio — In Progress**.

After completing a lab, describe only the work you personally performed and can explain in an interview. Use actual findings and numbers from your report. Reference answers and prepared instructions are not evidence that you completed an exercise.
